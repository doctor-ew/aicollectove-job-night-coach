# Supplied wording reference — W1

This note is part of the Jobs Night kit. A builder inspected [Resume Genius, “250+ Action Verbs to Make Your Resume Stand Out”](https://resumegenius.com/blog/resume-help/action-verbs) on 2026-09-22, specifically Key takeaways and the teamwork, organizational and technical sections. A host receiving this note has read this summary, not necessarily the live page.

Summary: Begin bullets with a concrete action. Choose vocabulary relevant to the work. Replace vague descriptions with a precise contribution. Numbers belong only where the candidate has evidence for them.

## Organizer editorial guidance

These distinctions are kit-authored guidance, not quotations or definitions attributed to Resume Genius:

Preparing a document or schedule: Drafted or prepared. Use the verb that matches what the seeker actually did.

Helping someone else: Supported or assisted. Do not turn help into leadership without evidence.

Performing a check: Checked or reviewed. Do not call it an audit or certification unless that was the actual work.

Investigating a cause: Investigated or diagnosed, when supported. Do not add prevention or measurable improvement.

Building something: Built or developed. Name the actual thing and tools without inventing wider scope.

## Stronger verbs

Kit-authored organizer editorial guidance, not quotations or rankings from Resume Genius. Use a stronger verb only when the seeker's own resume text or answer shows the matching role. If the evidence supports only the baseline verb, ask before offering the stronger one.

| What the evidence shows | Baseline | Stronger, with the evidence it needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Building something | Built, Developed | Engineered: the seeker did the hands-on technical work; the seeker's own "built" or "implemented" wording already shows this. Designed or Architected: the seeker chose the structure or approach. Launched or Delivered: it went into use. |
| Preparing a document or schedule | Drafted, Prepared | Authored: the seeker wrote it. Standardized: the seeker made a format others now follow. |
| Automating a task | Automated | Streamlined: the seeker made the task simpler. Eliminated: the seeker removed a manual step. |
| Helping someone else | Supported, Assisted | Partnered with or Contributed to: the seeker worked alongside others on a shared piece. Led, Directed or Spearheaded: only when the seeker confirms leading people. |
| Performing a check | Checked, Reviewed | Validated or Verified: the seeker tested against a stated standard. Evaluated: the seeker weighed options. Audited or Certified: only when that was the actual work. |
| Investigating a cause | Investigated, Diagnosed | Pinpointed or Traced: the seeker found the specific cause. Resolved: the seeker confirms the problem was fixed. |

Sentence shape: verb, then the specific object, then the purpose or confirmed result. Cut filler adjectives. Do not stack abstract nouns.

No verb implies a measurable improvement by itself. A stronger-sounding word is not automatically a more accurate word, so choose the strongest word the evidence supports and no stronger. Cite the resume or answer for what happened; cite this note separately for the wording rationale.
